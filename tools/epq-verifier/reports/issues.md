## a1-mnist-digit-classification

*Can a no-code image classifier read handwritten digits, and can you explain its mistakes?*

### INTEG-03 — Implies more than the data can show

*med · integ*

> If all three produce similar results, the features are doing most of the work.

**Why:** This invites the student to write up a causal/general conclusion about the feature set from learner agreement on one dataset and one split, without noting that all three learners may simply share the same blind spots or that the comparison says nothing about how good the features are.

**Suggested:** Hedge it and add the check: say that similar results suggest learner choice matters less than the feature set here, but that the student must also check whether the three learners fail on the same images before drawing that conclusion.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.5.2 AO2 Use Resources: “Analyse data apply relevantly and demonstrate understanding of any links, connections and complexities of the topic.”</sub>

### INTEG-02 — Sources named too vaguely to reference

*low · integ*

> If you want more training data, full MNIST is available from Yann LeCun's page and from [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ylecun/mnist).

**Why:** 'Yann LeCun's page' is a homepage reference with no stable URL, date or version, so a student citing it will produce exactly the kind of vague bibliography entry that cannot show critical selection of sources for AO2; the further-reading entry for the same dataset is also undated and unlinked.

**Suggested:** Replace with a full citation, e.g. LeCun, Cortes and Burges (1998), 'The MNIST Database of Handwritten Digits', http://yann.lecun.com/exdb/mnist/, and name the licence so the student can record it.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.2 The Taught Element: “referencing, the evaluation of sources and the prevention of plagiarism”</sub>

## a2-cloud-image-classification

*Can a model trained on curated cloud photographs recognise the sky above your school?*

### ETH-01 — People become data subjects without ethics appearing

*high · ethics*

> Their answers are data about a person: get the centre's approval first, keep the responses anonymous, and say so on the sheet.

**Why:** It tells students to collect classifications from a teacher or friend and mentions centre approval and anonymity, but not consent or the right to withdraw; participants could become data subjects without agreeing to take part.

**Suggested:** Add a requirement to ask participants for consent and tell them they can withdraw at any time, alongside the existing centre approval and anonymity instructions.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-PROJECTS-QG-ALL.PDF — Quick guide: ethical principles for the EPQ: “assessing the ethical principles of project proposals and research”</sub>

## a3-local-turing-test

*Can classmates tell BritLLM's writing from a human's when the human is imitating the machine?*

### AI-01 — Could be read as permission to let the model do the work

*high · ai*

> You could analyse the results in a spreadsheet or in Python.

**Why:** The project already puts a language model on the same machine, and this invites code-based analysis without saying the code and report must be the student's own work or that any AI assistance must be declared.

**Suggested:** Add an explicit note that the analysis code and written report must be the student's own work, and that any AI assistance must be declared and must not generate the submission.

<sub>https://www.aqa.org.uk/files/345f3433-b1f8-4406-93a6-19dd85874079/445389386589a6083669db126a9e792a1fae35cc.pdf — AQA AI guidance for EPQ (2025), introduction: “Centre Coordinators/Supervisors must only accept work for project work which is the learners' own.”</sub>

### ETH-01 — People become data subjects without ethics appearing

*high · ethics*

> If you ask classmates, get their consent, keep the texts anonymous, and do not collect anything more personal than the writing itself.

**Why:** This covers consent and anonymity for classmates' written contributions, but it does not say those contributions need centre ethics approval or that contributors can withdraw their texts; the later approval/withdrawal sentence is explicitly about judging sessions only.

**Suggested:** Add that written contributions from classmates also require centre approval and a right to withdraw before analysis, not just consent and anonymity.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-PROJECTS-QG-ALL.PDF — Quick guide: ethical principles for the EPQ: “assessing the ethical principles of project proposals and research”</sub>

## b1-quantisation-comparison

*How much memory, speed and energy does quantisation save, and what does it cost in quality?*

### ETH-03 — Practical risk is never mentioned

*med · ethics*

> A plug-in power meter is an alternative, but it measures the whole machine and is harder to attribute to the model.

**Why:** The page offers a mains-powered device, and `powermetrics` with administrator rights, as energy-measurement options without any mention of a risk assessment, so a student could set up unfamiliar equipment or run privileged commands without supervisor sign-off or a recorded risk plan.

**Suggested:** Add a sentence requiring a risk assessment and supervisor approval before using a power meter or administrator-level tools, and point the student to the centre's risk-assessment procedure.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.2 The Taught Element: “conducting risk assessment, assessing the ethical principles of project proposals and research”</sub>

### INTEG-02 — Sources named too vaguely to reference

*low · integ*

> The [MLX documentation](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm) on quantisation and `mlx_lm.convert` — the practical reference for the models you are using.

**Why:** The reference is given without a version, commit or access date, and the MLX API changes between releases; a student citing it cannot show the critical selection of sources that AO2 rewards.

**Suggested:** Add the version or commit hash and an access date, or link to a versioned documentation page, so the student can cite it precisely.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.2 The Taught Element: “referencing, the evaluation of sources and the prevention of plagiarism”</sub>

## b2-language-model-failure-and-confidence

*Can a small language model tell you when it is likely to be wrong?*

### FEAS-02 — Assumes kit or software without flagging it

*med · feas*

> You would run [BritLLM](https://huggingface.co/britllm/britllm-3b-v0.1), the 3B model you would run locally, on a set of questions with known answers.

**Why:** Running the model locally means downloading its weights and having enough disk, but the page only flags the Mac/16 GB and mlx-lm; a student could discover the download and storage requirement only after choosing the project.

**Suggested:** Add an explicit note of the approximate download size and disk space needed, or point to the Prerequisites page for storage/download requirements before the student commits.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.4 Assessing the Potential of a Proposal: “Are the working title and proposed action clear and focused on an issue which can be managed within the timescale, available resources and word total?”</sub>

### INTEG-02 — Sources named too vaguely to reference

*low · integ*

> For a general measure of the model's surprise, use a held-out text: a public-domain book chapter or a Wikipedia extract.

**Why:** This names a category rather than a citable source; a student could list 'a Wikipedia extract' or 'a book chapter' in the bibliography without title, URL, date or publisher.

**Suggested:** Give a specific example text with full citation details, or require students to record title, author/editor, publisher, URL and access date for whichever text they choose.

<sub>https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/subjects/AQA-W-7993-SP-19.PDF — §2.2 The Taught Element: “referencing, the evaluation of sources and the prevention of plagiarism”</sub>
