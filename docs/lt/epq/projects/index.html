Data Science and AI EPQ project examples

The examples below are designed to help you envisage what a Data Science and AI EPQ project might look like, from developing a research question and choosing suitable data to building and evaluating a model. They are intended as inspiration rather than projects to copy. An important part of the EPQ is developing and taking ownership of your own project, so you should choose a question that interests you and develop your own approach to investigating it.

Each project page follows the same pattern:

  • Project overview
  • Dataset(s) to be used
  • Suggested research question or hypothesis
  • Key data science, statistical and machine learning concepts
  • Suggested approaches to data preparation, analysis and modelling
  • Potential limitations, risks and sources of bias
  • Expected outcomes and opportunities for evaluation
  • Suitability as an EPQ artefact
  • Example artefacts
  • Further reading

The example artefacts are placeholders until the Orange workflows and Python plots are built.

A1–A3 and C1 are Orange projects. B1–B2 are Python projects: you would need to read and adapt scripts, run them from the terminal, and plot results. None of the six requires training a large model from random weights, and all six are designed for a 16 GB M-series Mac. You do not need to work through them in order.

Setting up

For A1–A3 and C1, you need Orange Data Mining with the orange3-ai-suite add-on installed. The add-on provides the Image Features, Image Pixels, LM Text Loader, Load LM, LM Pre-trainer, LM Generator and Neural Network Viewer widgets. For A1–A2, install the add-on’s image extra (OpenCV). A3 needs mlx-lm on Apple Silicon; the add-on’s lora extra installs it for loading BritLLM.

Data and models:

  • A1: the orange3-ai-suite repository includes a prepared MNIST subset, or you can download the full MNIST dataset.
  • A2: the orange3-ai-suite repository includes a cloud fetcher that downloads the WMO International Cloud Atlas corpus. You also collect your own sky photographs.
  • A3: BritLLM is available locally. Use public-domain texts, such as Project Gutenberg editions, or your own writing for the human side of the test.
  • C1: download the Gapminder indicators you need, and optionally cross-check them against the World Bank.
  • B1–B2: the local BritLLM MLX quantisations, plus Python 3.11 or later with mlx-lm, numpy, matplotlib and psutil.

Everything runs locally once the data and models are downloaded. The Orange add-on, the BritLLM model files and the datasets are external dependencies; they are not included in this pack.

A note on resources in B1

“Energy storage” can mean several things. B1 separates three resources:

  • Storage: the size of the model on disk, in megabytes or gigabytes.
  • Memory: the RAM used while the model is loaded and generating.
  • Energy: the joules consumed per token or per correct answer.

B1 gives you measurements for all three. You decide which of them matters for your use case, and argue the case from your results.
