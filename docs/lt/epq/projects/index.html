Data Science and AI EPQ project examples

The examples below are designed to help you envisage what a Data Science and AI EPQ project might look like, from developing a research question and choosing suitable data to building and evaluating a model. They are intended as inspiration rather than projects to copy. An important part of the EPQ is developing and taking ownership of your own project, so you should choose a question that interests you and develop your own approach to investigating it.

Each project page opens with what the project needs — machine, software and downloads — and then follows the same pattern:

  • Project overview
  • Dataset(s) to be used
  • Example research questions or hypothesis
  • Key data science, statistical and machine learning concepts
  • Example approaches to data preparation, analysis and modelling
  • Potential limitations, risks and sources of bias
  • Expected outcomes and opportunities for evaluation
  • Example artefacts
  • Further reading

Setting up

Each page says what it needs before it starts: the machine, the software and the downloads, with sizes. Prerequisites describes the two routes — Orange Data Mining for P1–P4, Python for P5–P6 — and the permissions that take time to arrange.

None of the six requires training a large model from random weights, and you do not need to work through them in order.

Everything runs locally once the data and models are downloaded. The Orange add-on, the BritLLM model files and the datasets are external dependencies; they are not included in this pack.

A note on resources in P5

“Energy storage” can mean several things. P5 separates three resources:

  • Storage: the size of the model on disk, in megabytes or gigabytes.
  • Memory: the RAM used while the model is loaded and generating.
  • Energy: the joules consumed per token or per correct answer.

P5 gives you measurements for all three. You decide which of them matters for your use case, and argue the case from your results.
